# Comparing Free AI Coding Tools: Antigravity, Windsurf, and OpenCode

AI coding tools promise to make building software faster—but the experience can vary a lot depending on the tool, model, and task. I’ve tried Antigravity, Windsurf, and OpenCode, mostly using their free options. Here’s how they’ve worked for me in practice.

> _Note: This is based on my personal experience, not a controlled benchmark. Free usage limits and paid prices can change over time._

## Antigravity: Fast, capable, but limited by usage

I first tried Antigravity a few months ago. At the time, it offered access to models like Gemini 3 and Claude 4.5. However, I quickly hit the free usage limit after sending just a few large prompts.

One standout feature for web projects was its virtual browser: it could preview changes live while I edited the code. Watching the results take shape in real time was a game-changer, and it also felt incredibly fast.

More importantly, Antigravity often seemed to understand the underlying problem—not just the immediate code change I requested—and produced solutions that aligned with my expectations.
It reminded me of working in a VS Code-based editor (and the Antigravity IDE is indeed derived from VS Code).

- **Best for:** People who want a fast editor that can reason through tasks and preview web changes.
- **Watch out for:** Free usage runs out quickly if you send large prompts.

## Windsurf: Handy for bugs, with a token-quota model

I mostly used Windsurf when I ran into bugs I didn’t feel like solving myself. While it previously used a credit-based system, Cognition (which acquired Windsurf in July 2025 and later rebranded the desktop app as **Devin Desktop**) replaced credits with daily and weekly token quotas. In my experience, I rarely hit the daily limit.

I primarily used the SWE-1.6 model. It was decent for reviewing and fixing issues, though it wasn’t always my go-to for more complex tasks. You’ll want to check Cognition's official Devin site for the latest product and plan details.

- **Best for:** Occasional bug-fixing and coding assistance.
- **Watch out for:** The unpublished token quotas and rebranding under Devin can make it harder to predict how much usage you get.

## OpenCode: My favorite workflow so far

OpenCode was the last tool I tried, and it’s become my favorite for building frontend features. Sometimes, it even comes up with more creative design ideas than I do—which is impressive since I’m not great at visualizing designs in my head. It’s surprised me with good results more than once. 🤣

That said, one frustration is that for small changes, it sometimes updates a lot of the codebase instead of making a narrow, localized fix. I wish it were more careful about keeping changes focused.

I also prefer its CLI (terminal UI) over its desktop GUI by a significant margin. The simple **Plan** and **Build** modes make the workflow feel intuitive and efficient. Overall, OpenCode’s workflow has been the strongest for me compared to the other tools I’ve tried.

- **Best for:** People who like working in the terminal and want a straightforward plan-then-build workflow.
- **Watch out for:** Review the diff carefully, especially when requesting small fixes.

---

> **A note on “free”:** Hosted tools like Antigravity and Windsurf offer free tiers with usage caps and limited model choices, whereas OpenCode is free and open-source software (you either bring your own API keys, pay per token via OpenCode Console, or buy an optional subscription). The table below lists published paid prices as a rough comparison—not a guarantee that every feature or model is included at that price.

## Which One Would I Choose?

- **Antigravity** if I want speed, strong problem-solving, and a live preview for web work.
- **Windsurf** if I need a convenient assistant for occasional bug fixes.
- **OpenCode** if I prefer a CLI-first workflow and a simple planning process.

> \*NOTE: I checked this only on free tier, probably on padi versions this tools give much better results.

I still plan to try Claude Code and Cursor to compare their results with these tools.
